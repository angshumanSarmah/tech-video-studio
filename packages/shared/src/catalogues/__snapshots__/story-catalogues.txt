## TOPIC CLASSES (exhaustive: use only these ids)
- product-design (Product design): How a user-facing feature works end to end: requests through services, places, stock and time. Examples: URL shortener, ride matching, seat booking, delivery ETA. Layout modes, most typical first: map, pipeline, hub_spoke, grid, swimlane, centered_focus, versus_split, near_far, two_column, stacked_list. Preferred devices: persona_hook, contention_hook, hero_metric, object_to_data, failure_before_fix, named_traps, seen_this_bug, ownership_twist, race_reveal, real_world_closure, callback_ending.
- internals (Internals): How one piece of infrastructure works inside: its data structures, storage and request path. Examples: how a key-value store keeps keys, database indexes, log partitions. Layout modes, most typical first: centered_focus, stacked_list, versus_split, pipeline, grid, swimlane, two_column, hub_spoke, near_far, cycle. Preferred devices: semantic_zoom, question_chapters, what_breaks, versus_split, brute_force_first, planted_question, interview_hook, real_incident.
- concept (Concept): One idea that recurs across systems, shown on one small concrete example. Examples: caching, consistent hashing, idempotency, rate limiting. Layout modes, most typical first: near_far, versus_split, cycle, grid, centered_focus, pipeline, hub_spoke, two_column, stacked_list, swimlane. Preferred devices: failure_before_fix, brute_force_first, versus_split, what_breaks, seen_this_bug, counterfactual, contrast_takeaway, real_incident, interview_hook.
- ai-ml (AI and ML): How a model or AI system works: what goes in, what it computes, why the output looks that way. Examples: tokenization, retrieval-augmented generation, KV cache, embeddings. Layout modes, most typical first: pipeline, cycle, versus_split, centered_focus, stacked_list, two_column, grid, hub_spoke, near_far. Preferred devices: object_to_data, planted_question, what_breaks, brute_force_first, versus_split, semantic_zoom, question_chapters, counterfactual, seen_this_bug.

## NARRATIVE DEVICES (exhaustive: use only these ids)
- persona_hook (Persona hook): Open on one relatable person in an everyday moment that quietly depends on the system. Use: A user-facing product whose load or failure is felt by ordinary people. Avoid: Internals or pure concepts with no natural person in the story.
- contention_hook (Contention hook): Open on many contenders racing for one scarce resource at the same instant. Use: Locks, inventory, seats, slots, quotas: anything where many want one. Avoid: Nothing is scarce, or the contention is not the core problem.
- interview_hook (Interview hook): Open on the question an interviewer would ask about this topic, then answer it. Use: Classic system design or ML interview topics engineers revise for. Avoid: Niche internals nobody is asked about; use a number-led hook instead.
- planted_question (Planted question, late payoff): Raise a question early, leave it open, answer it near the end. Fill `plantedQuestion`. Use: The topic has one non-obvious "but how?" that the mechanism finally explains. Avoid: The answer is needed early to follow the rest, or the video is very short.
- question_chapters (Question-driven chapters): Each chapter is titled as a question the viewer would ask; the chapter answers it. Use: Two to four distinct sub-problems that each deserve a heading. Avoid: One continuous mechanism; a roadmap or no chapters reads better.
- roadmap (Roadmap of steps): Show the numbered steps up front, then walk through them with a step tracker. Use: A process with a fixed order of 3 to 7 steps. Avoid: The order is the surprise, or there are fewer than 3 steps.
- recap_opening (Recap opening): Open a later part with a recap of at most two sentences, then its own hook. Use: Part 2 or later of a multi-part video. Avoid: A single video or part 1.
- hero_metric (Hero metric / story clock): Pin one number for the whole video, optionally a running clock in story time. Fill `heroMetric`. Use: One latency, deadline or budget that every step spends from. Avoid: No single number ties the steps together.
- object_to_data (Object-to-data morph): A real-world thing turns into the record or message the system actually sees. Use: The first time a physical event becomes data (a scan, a tap, a sensor reading). Avoid: Everything in the story is already data.
- failure_before_fix (Failure before fix): Show the naive design breaking under load or at an edge, then the fix. Use: The fix only makes sense once the failure is seen. Avoid: There is no plausible naive design to break.
- brute_force_first (Brute force before clever fix): Do it the slow obvious way first, show the cost, then the structure that avoids it. Use: An index, a hash, a grid or a cache replaces a scan over everything. Avoid: The obvious way is not actually slow at the scale in the notes.
- named_traps (Named traps): Give each edge case a short memorable name and show it one at a time. Use: Two or three distinct edge cases the design must survive. Avoid: Only one edge case; show it as failure before fix instead.
- seen_this_bug (You've seen this bug): Start from a glitch the viewer has met as a user, then explain what caused it. Use: A familiar symptom (stale count, double charge, vanished item) maps to the mechanism. Avoid: The symptom is invisible to users.
- ownership_twist (Ownership twist): Reveal that the data or decision is owned by a different system than assumed. Use: The source of truth is surprising and changes how the design works. Avoid: Ownership is obvious or irrelevant to the mechanism.
- race_reveal (Race reveal (two clocks)): Two timers run at once and the outcome depends on which ends first. Use: Timeouts, leases, holds and retries racing each other. Avoid: Only one timer matters.
- versus_split (Versus split): Two designs or outcomes for the same situation, side by side. Use: A trade-off between two named options with the same input. Avoid: More than two options, or the options share nothing to compare.
- what_breaks (What-breaks simulation): Remove or overload one part and show step by step what fails downstream. Use: Explaining why a component exists by taking it away. Avoid: Nothing visible fails; the cost is only slower in a way not shown.
- counterfactual (Counterfactual ("what if")): Show what would happen with a different choice, with its cost, then return. Use: A tempting alternative whose cost is concrete (time, money, errors). Avoid: The alternative is already the failure before fix; do not show it twice.
- semantic_zoom (Semantic zoom): Push into one component of the board to reveal its insides on the next board. Use: Going from the whole system to the one part that does the work. Avoid: The inside is not worth a board of its own.
- real_incident (Real incident case study): Concept first, then a real dated outage from the notes, with its source shown. Use: The notes hold an incident with a date and a publisher. Avoid: The notes have no sourced incident; never invent or blur one.
- real_world_closure (Real-world closure): End on the physical moment where the system finally touches the user. Use: A flow that starts digital and ends in the real world. Avoid: The result never leaves the screen.
- contrast_takeaway (Two-line contrast takeaway): Close a section on two short lines: what is easy, and what is actually hard. Use: The core insight is a contrast the viewer should repeat. Avoid: The insight is a single fact, not a contrast.
- callback_ending (Callback ending + share CTA): Return to the opening image or person with the problem now solved, then a share line. Use: The hook had a person or image worth coming back to. Avoid: The hook was a bare number with nothing to call back to.
- spec_card_ending (Spec card ending): End on a 3-line spec card summarising the design, plus the series number. Use: The last part of every video: the house ending. Combine with a callback if used. Avoid: Earlier parts of a multi-part video; they end on a cliffhanger.
- cliffhanger_ending (Cliffhanger ending): End an earlier part on a line that names the next part's question, unanswered. Use: Every part but the last of a multi-part video. Avoid: A single video or the last part.

## LAYOUT MODES (exhaustive: use only these ids)
- map: A map base filling the stage, overlays sharing its frame, markers on top. Use: Places, distance or territory matter: riders, zones, regions, data centres. Zones: base, overlay, marker, aside. Max elements: 6.
- two_column: Two columns of elements, read left then right. Use: Two related groups read in order, such as inputs then outputs. Zones: left, right, aside. Max elements: 8.
- grid: Equal elements in a grid of cells. Use: Many equal items: shards, buckets, slots, replicas. Zones: cell, aside. Max elements: 12.
- stacked_list: Elements stacked top to bottom as rows. Use: An ordered list or stack: layers, log entries, ranked results. Zones: row, aside. Max elements: 6.
- centered_focus: One focus element in the middle, the rest smaller around it. Use: One thing is the subject and the rest is context. The default when nothing fits. Zones: center, around, aside. Max elements: 7.
- pipeline: Stages in flow order (top-down or left-right): sources, middle stages, sinks. Use: Something moves through stages in order: a request path, a data or model pipeline. Zones: source, stage, sink, aside. Max elements: 10.
- cycle: Steps on a ring that loops back to the start, an optional element in the middle. Use: A loop that repeats: retries, a training step, an agent calling tools in turn. Zones: ring, center, aside. Max elements: 8.
- swimlane: Vertical lanes, one per actor, with ordered messages between them. Use: Two to four actors exchange messages over time: a handshake, a protocol, a race. Zones: lane-1, lane-2, lane-3, lane-4, message, aside. Max elements: 12.
- versus_split: Two halves side by side for the same situation; shared elements span both. Use: Two options or two outcomes for the same input, compared side by side. Zones: left, right, shared. Max elements: 8.
- near_far: An origin with a near and a far destination, the distance drawn to scale. Use: The cost is distance or latency: a nearby cache versus a far database. Zones: origin, near, far, aside. Max elements: 5.
- hub_spoke: One hub in the middle with spokes around it. Use: One central thing serves or coordinates many: a broker, a coordinator, a lock. Zones: hub, spoke, aside. Max elements: 9.

## METAPHOR WORLDS (exhaustive: use only these ids; optional, at most one per video)
- traffic (Traffic): Roads, junctions and toll gates: requests as cars competing for lanes. Use: Load, throttling, routing and congestion. Avoid: Storage or data layout, where nothing moves. Maps: request = car; server capacity = lanes on a road; load balancer = officer directing a junction; rate limit = toll gate letting a fixed number through per minute; queue = line of cars at a signal; overload = traffic jam; retry with backoff = driver waiting longer before trying again.
- kitchen (Kitchen): A restaurant kitchen: orders as tickets, cooks as workers, the pantry as storage. Use: Work queues, worker pools, batching, caching prepared results. Avoid: Networks spread over distance; nothing in a kitchen is far away. Maps: request = order ticket; worker = cook; queue = ticket rail; cache = prepped ingredients on the counter; database = walk-in pantry; batching = cooking several orders in one pan; timeout = a dish sent back cold.
- library (Library): Shelves, catalogues and branches: data as books, lookups as finding a shelf. Use: Indexes, caches, sharding, replication and lookups. Avoid: Real-time flows where order and timing are the point. Maps: record = book; index = card catalogue; full scan = walking every aisle; cache = books kept on the front desk; shard = a separate branch holding some of the books; replica = a copy of a book in another branch; eviction = least-borrowed books sent to storage.
- factory (Factory): Conveyors and workstations: data as items moving through stages. Use: Pipelines, stream processing, backpressure and throughput. Avoid: Single lookups with no stages. Maps: pipeline stage = workstation; message = item on the conveyor; worker pool = workers sharing one station; backpressure = conveyor stopping when packing is full; batch = crate of items; failed job = item pulled off the line for inspection.
- post_office (Post office): Letters, sorting offices and postcodes: messages routed to their readers. Use: Message brokers, pub/sub, delivery guarantees and routing. Avoid: Synchronous calls where the caller waits for the answer. Maps: message = letter; broker = sorting office; partition = sorting bin; routing key = postcode; at-least-once delivery = registered mail resent until signed for; dead-letter queue = shelf for undeliverable mail; publish-subscribe = newsletter posted to every subscriber.
- detective_office (Detective office): Evidence boards and witness statements: debugging as an investigation. Use: Observability, logs, traces, root-cause analysis and incidents. Avoid: Explaining how a system works when nothing has gone wrong. Maps: log line = witness statement; trace = map of the suspect's movements; metrics dashboard = evidence board; alert = tip-off call; hypothesis = suspect; root cause = culprit.
- star_map (Star map): A night sky of points: meaning as position, similarity as distance. Use: Embeddings, vector search, clustering and nearest neighbours. Avoid: Anything without a notion of distance between items. Maps: embedding = a star's position; vector space = the night sky; similarity = distance between stars; nearest-neighbour search = finding the closest stars to a point; cluster = constellation; query = a new point of light.
